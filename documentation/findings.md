# Findings

All numbers regenerated by `code/00_main.R` into `output/*.csv`; independently re-derived from the raw
source in the verification pass (see `reflections.md`). n = 19 survey respondents — read counts, treat
percentages as indicative.

## Part I — Demand (the survey)

### Capacity (Q6) — most focal points cannot model in-house
| Staff band | n | % |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0 staff | 13 | 68 |
| 1–5 staff | 5 | 26 |
| >10 staff | 1 | 5 |

Two-thirds of institutes have **no** mathematical modeller; one has a team of any size. This is the
whole reason ECDC forecasting matters here — for most countries it substitutes for a capability they
do not possess.

### Engagement with ECDC outputs (Q5, mean 0–5, ordinal)
| Output | Class | Mean | % ≥4 | % not aware |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| COVID-19 risk assessments | Guidance | 3.89 | 68 | 5 |
| COVID-19 Forecast Hub | Forecasting | 2.72 | 33 | 17 |
| COVID-19 Scenario Hub | Scenario | 2.69 | 25 | 12 |
| RespiCast | Forecasting | 2.11 | 22 | 17 |
| RespiCompass | Scenario | 1.35 | 6 | 29 |

Engagement declines from the oldest, most human-readable output (guidance) to the newest hubs.
**Caveat:** this gradient tracks *time in market* (guidance since 2020, RespiCast 2023, RespiCompass
2024), so it is not a value ranking.

### Value: forecasts vs scenarios (Q8)
| Choice | n | % |
|---|---:|---:|
| Both will be useful | 10 | 53 |
| RespiCast (forecasts) | 5 | 26 |
| Neither | 3 | 16 |
| RespiCompass (scenarios) | 1 | 5 |

A majority expect both to be useful; forced to pick one, forecasts beat scenarios **5:1**. This is a
stated *expectation* — several respondents note they have not yet used the outputs.

### The value story: near-universal need, flat uptake
Mean engagement by class × capacity band shows short-term forecasting essentially **flat** across
capacity (0 staff 2.42 · 1–5 2.25 · >10 3.0, the last n=1). The institutes that would gain most do not
engage more. So the value is **potential, not yet realised**: supply is not the bottleneck (Part II) —
demand-side friction is (awareness, interpretation, integration).

### Decisions modelling would inform (Q7, % likely / very likely)
| Action | % likely | % unlikely |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inform surveillance activities | 58 | 26 |
| Planning vaccination campaigns | 37 | 37 |
| Procuring medical countermeasures | 26 | 42 |
| Healthcare capacity planning | 16 | 42 |

Value concentrates in **situational awareness** (surveillance); the canonical high-stakes forecasting
use case — **healthcare-capacity planning** — is the *least* likely to be informed. Worth reporting as
a limit on decision impact, not burying.

### Integration mechanism (Q10)
Agree + strongly agree **9/19 (47%)**; disagree + strongly disagree **6/19 (32%)**; neutral 4/19. Only
about half report a clear route to integrate modelling into decisions — a ceiling on realised value.

### Communication channels (Q11/Q12, mean rank, 1 = best)
For **weekly forecasts (RespiCast)**: newsletter/mailing list (1.79) ≈ dedicated website (2.00) lead;
analyst meetings (2.89) and policy-maker meetings (3.32) trail. For **scenarios (RespiCompass)** the
website (2.16) and newsletter (2.32) lead, with analyst meetings (2.53) closer behind. Self-serve
channels beat meetings for both; policy-maker meetings rank last throughout.

### Voices (open responses, theme-coded)
The strongest themes (by respondents): a **structural capacity gap** (no/outsourced modelling staff,
explicit asks for ECDC to recommend and fund national capacity); an **awareness/adoption gap** (outputs
known but not embedded, adoption deferred until a season's track record); **trust conditional on
demonstrated skill** (short-term trusted over long-horizon scenarios; requests for transparent
performance metrics and fewer models); benchmark-only use by resourced NFPs; and an
**interpretability/training gap** (outputs must be usable by non-modellers). Full synthesis in
`output/themes.json`.

## Part II — Delivery (the hubs)

### Indicators, windows, coverage
| Indicator | Hub | Rounds | Distinct models | Peak models | Countries |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| COVID-19 hospitalisations | RespiCast-Covid19 | 88 | 17 | 11 | 15 |
| ILI incidence | RespiCast-SyndromicIndicators | 91 | 25 | 20 | 30 |
| ARI incidence | RespiCast-SyndromicIndicators | 91 | 18 | 14 | 25 |

91 weekly rounds span two seasons (2024/25, 2025/26). The **ensemble** is present in ~98% of rounds
(89/91 ILI & ARI; 87/88 COVID).

### The ILI–ARI asymmetry
ILI and ARI live in the **same hub, in the same files**, yet ILI draws roughly twice the models
(median 14 vs 7) and reaches more countries (30 vs 25): six syndromic teams submit ILI-only in a given
round. Coverage breadth is an indicator-level choice by teams, not a hub-level constraint.

### Temporal texture
Participation swells each winter (Nov–Mar) and thins over summer without stopping — the hubs run
year-round at reduced cadence. Season *means* are roughly flat across the two years (e.g. ILI 12.3 →
11.4), though winter *peaks* rose (ILI 16 → 20, ARI 9 → 14). The mid-2026 tail (1–2 models) is
off-season / incomplete current weeks, not a coverage collapse.

### Participation & churn
Across both hubs, a spine of stalwarts (ECDC-SARIMA, ISI-*, the ensembles and baseline) submits nearly
every round, while other teams arrive mid-season, appear for a single winter, or rotate in and out —
visible as the participation ribbon in the dashboard.
