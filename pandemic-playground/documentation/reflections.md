## 2026-07 — What the playground is actually for: scoring, and its limits

The instrument's value is not the simulator and not the tools individually — it is the **pairing**: an
answer next to the truth it tried to recover. Three things follow.

### 1. The recurring lesson is "correct on truth, biased on observed"

Several tools are *exactly* right on the latent truth and *wrong* on the observed data, and the wrongness
is the point:

| Tool | Exact on… | Biased on observed because… |
|---|---|---|
| Phase-0 growth → R | true infections (recovers R=2.6) | ascertainment ramps up → apparent growth inflated |
| Phase-1 Cori Rt | source true infections (cor ~0.99) | onset-date lag; importation read as local transmission |
| Phase-1 CFR (adjusted) | the confirmed CFR | case under-ascertainment uncorrected → overstates IFR |
| Phase-2 forecast | growth/decline phases | fixed R can't see the turnover; onset-R lag |

A tool that is *unbiased in principle* can be *badly biased in practice* purely through the observation
process. This is the single most useful thing a truth-aware testbed can teach, and it is why the
observation model is not an afterthought but half the code. **[REFLECTION]** The natural next study is to
turn each bias into a *correction* and re-score: deconvolve onsets to infection dates before Cori
(removes the lag); model the ascertainment ramp explicitly (removes the growth inflation); anchor the
CFR on a seroprevalence estimate of ascertainment (recovers the IFR). The playground is set up to say,
quantitatively, how much each correction buys.

### 2. A good score here is necessary, not sufficient, for trust on real data

Every tool is scored against a truth generated by *the same structural assumptions the tool makes*: a
renewal process with a known generation interval, Poisson/multinomial observation, a fixed IFR. So a
tool doing well here means it is **internally correct** — it recovers the truth when its assumptions
hold. It does **not** mean it will do well on real data, where the assumptions are only approximate
(spatial structure, behavioural change, heterogeneous delays, misspecified generation intervals).
**[REFLECTION]** The honest use of the playground is therefore (a) as a *correctness* gate — a tool that
fails here is broken — and (b) as a *robustness* rig: deliberately break an assumption in the DGP (make
the generation interval time-varying, add a second importation source, let ascertainment jump) and see
which tools degrade gracefully and which fall over. Robustness, not accuracy under matched assumptions,
is what predicts real-world usefulness. This is the same precautionary instinct as the parent flu
project's insistence that a national ILI+ curve is a spatial overlay a single SIR mis-specifies.

### 3. The deaths-thinning choice is a modelling stance worth stating

Deaths are drawn as an *independent* thinning of infections, not as a subset of confirmed cases (see
`decisions.md`). The consequence is exact and deliberate: the delay-adjusted CFR converges to
`IFR × death_detection / ascertainment`, so it overstates the IFR by exactly the case-ascertainment
gap. **[REFLECTION]** This makes the playground a clean demonstration of *why the early-pandemic CFR is
not the IFR* — the two biases (delay, ascertainment) are separable and only the first is corrected by the
Nishiura adjustment. A line-list mode where deaths are a subset of cases would be more faithful to some
data systems and is the obvious alternative to offer; it would change the CFR interpretation and is
noted as a front.

## 2026-07 — On single-population-per-country, and honest heterogeneity

Each country is one well-mixed renewal epidemic. The parent flu project argues at length that national
curves are really spatial overlays of many local epidemics with space- and time-varying reporting, and
that a single-population model mis-specifies that. The same caveat applies here — but the *purpose*
differs: we are not trying to reconstruct a real country's dynamics, we are generating a controlled
truth to test tools. **[REFLECTION]** Still, the most valuable single extension for *robustness* testing
would be exactly this: a metapopulation per country (a few sub-regions with staggered importation and
their own delays), so that nowcasting and Rt tools can be stressed on aggregated multi-local signals
rather than a single clean wave. That would let the playground reproduce, and then test tools against,
the very effect the parent project treats as fundamental.

## Open questions / next steps

- **Turn biases into corrections and re-score** (deconvolution before Rt; ascertainment model; sero-
  anchored IFR). The headline experiments the scoring layer is built for.
- **Robustness rig:** systematically misspecify one DGP assumption at a time and rank tools by graceful
  degradation, not matched-assumption accuracy.
- **A line-list / subset-deaths observation mode**, and its effect on the CFR interpretation.
- **Metapopulation countries** (sub-regional overlay) to test aggregation effects.
- **Ensemble scoring across many seeds:** every score here is one stochastic realisation; repeating over
  seeds turns "the nowcast cut RMSE 40–85%" into a distribution with a confidence statement.
- **Age structure** in the renewal (with a contact matrix) so the age-structured IFR and age-specific
  severity questions become answerable rather than collapsed.
