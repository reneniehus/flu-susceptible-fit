What do national focal points get from ECDC forecasting — and what do the hubs actually deliver?

Two views of the same system. Demand: a survey of 19 EU/EEA National Focal Points on their in-house modelling capacity and the value they place on ECDC's short-term forecasts (RespiCast) and scenarios (RespiCompass). Delivery: two seasons of the RespiCast forecasting hubs, reconstructed submission-by-submission — which indicators were forecast, in which weeks, by how many models and the ensemble.

Part I

Demand — the value of forecasting to national focal points

The survey went to National Focal Points (NFPs) for viral respiratory diseases — one collective response per country. It asked how much modelling they can do themselves, how much they engage with each ECDC output, which decisions those outputs might inform, and which of the two flagship products (forecasts vs scenarios) they expect to be most useful.

Most focal points cannot model in-house — so ECDC is often the only forecast they have

Q6 · Mathematical-modelling staff in the national institute, irrespective of disease area. Each block is one of the 19 respondents.

Awareness & engagement with ECDC outputs

Q5 · Self-rated engagement, 0 (not aware) → 5 (actively used); an ordinal scale, so read the ranking, not the absolute. Newer products sit lower — but note they have had far less time in market (guidance since 2020, RespiCast 2023, RespiCompass 2024), so recency, not lesser value, explains much of the gap.

Which output will be most valuable?

Q8 · RespiCast (weekly short-term forecasts) vs RespiCompass (seasonal scenarios). Most expect both to be useful; when forced to pick one, forecasts win by a wide margin. This is stated expectation — many respondents note they have not yet used the outputs.

The value story: near-universal need, but engagement is flat across capacity

Q5 × Q6 · Mean engagement with each output class, split by in-house modelling staff. The striking pattern is a non-pattern: institutes with no team of their own — the majority, and those who would gain most — engage with ECDC forecasting no more than better-resourced ones. The need is near-universal; the uptake is not. (The >10-staff band is a single institute — read it lightly.)

Which decisions would modelling inform?

Q7 · Likelihood that ECDC modelling informs each national action, ordered by share "likely". Situational awareness (surveillance, 58%) leads — but the canonical forecasting use case, healthcare-capacity planning, is the least likely (16%). Value concentrates in awareness, not high-stakes resource decisions.

Is there a mechanism to use the modelling?

Q10 · "There is a clear, consistent mechanism for integrating modelling outputs into public-health decisions at my institute." Only about half agree — a ceiling on realised value.

How focal points want forecasts delivered

Q11 / Q12 · Preferred communication channels, ranked (1 = most preferred). Mean rank across respondents — lower is better. A dedicated website and a newsletter beat meetings for both weekly forecasts and seasonal scenarios.

Voices from the field

Open responses (Q8b, Q13), independently theme-coded. What NFPs say about value, barriers and needs.

Where demand meets delivery

The survey measures appetite. The hubs are the meal actually being served.

Focal points told us short-term forecasting is what they most expect to value — yet even the countries with no modelling of their own engage only modestly. One explanation would be that the forecasts simply aren't there for them. So the natural next question is one of supply: over the last two seasons, what did the ECDC RespiCast hubs actually produce — for which indicators, in which weeks, and with how many models behind each ensemble? If supply turns out to be robust, then the gap between value and use lives on the demand side, not the supply side.

Reconstructed from every submission in the two hubs — RespiCast-Covid19 (COVID-19 hospital admissions) and RespiCast-SyndromicIndicators (ILI and ARI incidence). COVID-19 hospitalisations sit in their own hub, split off from the syndromic indicators; the syndromic hub has only ever carried ILI and ARI. "Models" counts genuine teams; the ensemble and the reference baseline are counted apart.

Each column is one weekly round. Cell shade = number of contributing models; the thin strip below each indicator marks weeks the ensemble was published. Hover any cell for the exact count, ensemble status and country reach. Coverage counts the presence of a submission — not its accuracy or skill.

Models per week — winter peaks, summer troughs, a persistent ILI–ARI gap

Contributing models per round, by indicator. ILI draws roughly twice the models of ARI in the same hub, from the same files; participation swells each winter and thins over summer without stopping. What this is

An independent analysis, not an ECDC publication. Built from a de-identified NFP survey export and the public RespiCast hub repositories. Every number here is regenerated from source by code/00_main.R; the charts read one embedded JSON produced by that pipeline.

Read carefully

19 respondents (of ~30 EU/EEA NFPs): every share moves ~5 points per person, so treat all percentages as indicative and read counts. Q5's 0–5 scale is ordinal (higher = more engaged); exact anchors aren't in the de-identified export. Q8 is a stated expectation, not observed use. Newer products have had less time in market. Coverage counts a submission's presence, not its skill; a country appearing in a panel does not mean its NFP uses the output; NFP awareness may understate national involvement where in-house teams already submit to the hubs.
